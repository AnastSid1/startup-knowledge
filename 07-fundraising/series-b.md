# Series B

> Series B is a later growth round that typically funds scaling a proven business model into larger markets, new segments, or expanded go-to-market capacity.

## What it means

By Series B, investors usually expect:

- a working revenue engine
- stronger management layers
- clearer competitive positioning
- path to durable advantage / efficient growth
- more sophisticated financial operations

Capital may fund international expansion, new product lines, enterprise motion buildout, or market share capture.

## Why it matters

Series B is less about “can this work?” and more about “how big and how efficiently can this scale?” Dilution, governance, and strategy choices compound significantly at this stage.

## When founders should care

- When Series A plan worked and the constraint is scale capital/talent
- When entering expensive markets (enterprise, multi-geo)
- When efficiency metrics matter as much as top-line growth

## How it works

Expectations often include:

- durable retention
- improving or stable unit economics at larger spend
- functional leaders in place or hireable
- credible multi-year plan

Valuation depends heavily on growth, efficiency, margins, and market conditions — see [valuation](valuation.md).

## Practical example

A company uses Series B to:

- launch EU enterprise sales
- invest in security/compliance for larger deals
- expand product from wedge workflow into adjacent suite
- build marketing brand in category

They can show that US mid-market motion already has predictable CAC payback.

## Common confusion

Series B is not automatically “success completed.” Many B-stage companies still have significant execution risk. Also, some companies raise out-of-sequence rounds or skip labels — focus on substance over letter.
