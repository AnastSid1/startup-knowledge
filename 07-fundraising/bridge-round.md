# Bridge Round

> A bridge round is interim financing meant to extend runway until a larger planned round or milestone — “bridging” a gap.

## What it means

Bridges can be:

- smaller SAFE/convertible raises
- insider rounds from existing investors
- short-term capital to finish a Series A/B process
- emergency financing when metrics or markets slip

A bridge is a tool, not a strategy. Sometimes it is healthy (process took longer). Sometimes it signals trouble (milestones missed).

## Why it matters

Bridges affect [dilution](dilution.md), investor signaling, and founder leverage. Done thoughtfully, they protect a company from raising a down round in panic. Done repeatedly, they can indicate structural problems.

## When founders should care

- When runway is shortening mid-process
- When a major milestone is weeks/months away and capital markets are slow
- When deciding between cutting burn vs raising a bridge vs both

## How it works

Common patterns:

1. **Inside bridge:** existing investors fund continuity
2. **SAFE bridge:** faster documentation, converts later
3. **Structured bridge:** may include discounts/caps reflecting risk

Questions to ask:

- What milestone unlocks the next real round?
- Is the bridge enough with buffer?
- What happens if the milestone slips again?
- How does this change the [cap table](cap-table.md)?

## Practical example

A startup has 4 months runway, Series A process ongoing, term sheets delayed by market conditions. Existing investors provide a bridge SAFE to add 8 months runway while cutting non-critical burn. The company closes A later without desperate terms.

Contrast: repeated bridges with flat metrics and no plan — usually a warning.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Extension | Sometimes used interchangeably |
| Down round | Priced round at lower valuation; bridge may try to avoid/postpone this |
| New seed | Fresh stage thesis; bridge is continuity capital |
