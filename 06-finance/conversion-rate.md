# Conversion Rate

> Conversion rate is the percentage of people who complete a desired action out of those who could have completed it.

## What it means

```text
Conversion rate = Completions / Eligible opportunities
```

Examples:

- visitor → signup
- signup → activated user
- trial → paid
- opportunity → closed-won
- free → team plan

Always specify the stage pair. “Our conversion rate is 3%” is meaningless alone.

## Why it matters

Conversion rates determine whether traffic and leads become a business. They also set [lead worth](../05-sales/lead-worth.md) and [CAC](cac.md).

Small conversion improvements compound across funnels.

## When founders should care

- Landing page and onboarding work
- Sales pipeline reviews
- Pricing/packaging experiments
- Channel quality comparisons

## How it works

Map a funnel with explicit denominators:

| Stage | Count | Conversion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors | 10,000 | — |
| Signups | 400 | 4.0% |
| Activated | 160 | 40% of signups |
| Paid | 32 | 20% of activated |

Diagnose the weakest meaningful step. Do not A/B test button colors if activation is the real cliff.

### Qualitative + quantitative

Talk to people who did not convert. Data shows where; conversations show why.

## Practical example

Trial → paid is 8%. Interviews reveal users hit value on day 10, but trial ends on day 7. Extending trial for activated users or accelerating time-to-value lifts conversion more than a new pricing page.

## Common confusion

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Mixing stages | Label each conversion |
| Optimizing early vanity conversion | Optimize for activated/paid quality |
| Comparing across unlike channels | Segment |
