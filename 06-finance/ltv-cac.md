# LTV:CAC

> LTV:CAC is the ratio of customer lifetime value to customer acquisition cost — a quick read on whether acquisition economics can work.

## What it means

```text
LTV:CAC = LTV / CAC
```

Example: LTV $3,200 / CAC $800 = **4.0x**.

Rule-of-thumb folklore in SaaS often cites ~3x as a healthy directional target. Reality depends on:

- gross margins
- growth goals
- payback timing
- capital availability
- retention uncertainty

A 3x ratio with 24-month payback can still be cash-painful. Always pair with [payback period](payback-period.md).

## Why it matters

LTV:CAC compresses two complex ideas into one decision metric for channel and segment strategy. It helps answer: “Should we spend more to grow this motion?”

## When founders should care

- Scaling paid or sales-led acquisition
- Comparing ICPs
- Fundraising efficiency narratives
- Killing zombie channels

## How it works

### Interpret with context

| Ratio | Possible reading |
| --- | --- |
| < 1x | Destroying value (as estimated) |
| ~1–2x | Fragile; improve retention/pricing or cut CAC |
| ~3x+ | Often workable for SaaS, if payback acceptable |
| Extremely high | Maybe underinvesting in growth — or LTV overestimated |

### Improve the ratio

- raise prices / expand revenue ([product worth](../01-product/product-worth.md))
- improve retention
- improve conversion
- focus on higher-quality channels
- reduce sales friction for the same ACV

## Practical example

Channel A: LTV $2,000, CAC $900 → 2.2x, payback 11 months  
Channel B: LTV $2,000, CAC $400 → 5.0x, payback 5 months

If capital is scarce, prefer B even if A can scale faster in vanity volume.

## Common confusion

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Using revenue LTV | Use gross-profit LTV |
| Blended ratio hiding bad segments | Segment |
| Ignoring time value / payback | Track both |
| Treating 3x as a law | Treat as heuristic |
