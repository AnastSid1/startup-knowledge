# Runway

> Runway is how many months your company can operate before cash runs out at the current burn rate.

## What it means

```text
Runway (months) ≈ Cash in bank / Net burn per month
```

Example: $1.8M cash / $150k net burn = **12 months runway**.

Runway changes when:

- burn changes
- revenue collections change
- you raise capital
- large one-off payments hit

## Why it matters

Runway is the company’s oxygen meter. It drives fundraising timing, hiring, and risk decisions.

A common operating heuristic: start fundraising early enough that you are not negotiating from desperation. Many founders aim to raise when they still have meaningful runway left (exact timing depends on market conditions).

## When founders should care

- Weekly/monthly as a core dashboard metric while unprofitable
- Before increasing burn
- When planning Seed / Series A timing
- If sales cycles lengthen and cash inflows slip

## How it works

### Scenario planning

| Scenario | Net burn | Cash | Runway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | $150k | $1.8M | 12 months |
| Hire faster | $220k | $1.8M | ~8 months |
| Cut paid spend | $110k | $1.8M | ~16 months |

Also model **revenue ramp** — runway can extend if collections rise.

### Fundraising interaction

If you need 6 months to raise and want 6 months post-raise buffer before the next cycle, your planning math must include that lead time. See [Bridge Round](../07-fundraising/bridge-round.md) if timing slips.

## Practical example

A startup has 9 months runway and wants to raise a Series A.

They decide:

- freeze non-critical hiring
- keep burn flat
- begin fundraising with ~8–9 months left rather than waiting for 3

This preserves negotiating leverage if the round takes longer.

## Common confusion

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Using gross burn only while revenue is material | Use net burn |
| Ignoring irregular cash timing | Maintain a cash calendar |
| Treating runway as precise to the day | Recompute monthly; use ranges |
