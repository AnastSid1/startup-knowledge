# Virality

> Virality is growth driven by users causing other people to discover or adopt a product through sharing, inviting, exposing, or embedding it.

## What it means

A product is viral when usage naturally creates exposure to non-users.

Mechanisms include:

- explicit invites (“invite your team”)
- shared artifacts (links, files, public profiles)
- communication that carries a signature (“sent via…”)
- word of mouth sparked by delight
- embedded widgets content

Virality is about **acquisition via users**. It is related to, but not the same as, [network effects](../03-market/network-effects.md) (value increases with users).

## Why it matters

Viral distribution can dramatically lower [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md) and accelerate learning. Some of the largest consumer and collaboration products were built on viral mechanics.

But forced virality (spammy invite walls) can harm trust and brand.

## When founders should care

- Building collaborative or communication products
- Designing [PLG](../01-product/product-led-growth.md) onboarding
- Looking for organic acquisition levers beyond content ads
- Measuring whether invites/shares are a real growth driver

## How it works

Track a simple viral system:

```text
Users → invites/shares → accepted invites → new activated users
```

Quantified with [viral coefficient](viral-coefficient.md) and cycle time.

Qualitative design questions:

- Why would a user invite someone?
- Is inviting required to get value, or optional and rewarding?
- Does the invite create value for the recipient immediately?

## Practical example

A scheduling tool becomes visible when a user sends booking links to others. Recipients experience the product before signing up. Some convert and create their own links.

This is product-borne distribution — virality through the core workflow, not a bolted-on referral banner.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Network effects | Value increases with network size |
| Referral program | Incentive-driven invites; see [referral loops](referral-loops.md) |
| Popularity | PR spikes are not structural virality |
| Retention | Separate; viral acquisition with poor retention fails |
