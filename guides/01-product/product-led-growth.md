# Product-Led Growth (PLG)

> Product-led growth is a go-to-market approach where the product itself is the main engine of acquisition, activation, conversion, and expansion.

## What it means

In a product-led company, users can often start with little or no sales involvement: self-serve signup, freemium, free trial, or usage-based access. The product teaches value, creates habit, and creates reasons to upgrade.

PLG is not “no sales.” Many PLG companies add sales later for qualified accounts (product-qualified leads). The point is that product usage — not only demos and SDRs — drives the funnel.

Typical PLG motions:

- freemium → paid upgrade
- free trial → conversion
- open core / community edition
- usage-based landing + expansion
- viral / collaborative product experiences

## Why it matters

When it works, PLG can produce:

- lower blended [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md)
- faster time-to-value
- broader top-of-funnel
- expansion revenue from usage and seats
- richer product data for roadmap and sales prioritization

It also forces product quality: if onboarding is confusing, growth stops. That feedback loop is healthy.

## When founders should care

PLG fits better when:

- users can get value quickly without heavy implementation
- individual users can start before a company-wide purchase
- the product is used frequently
- collaboration or sharing creates [network effects](../03-market/network-effects.md) or virality
- ACV starts low-to-mid and can expand

PLG fits worse when:

- buyers need heavy security review before any usage
- value only appears after months of integration
- purchases are top-down only and users cannot try alone
- margins cannot support a large free tier

Many modern companies are hybrid: PLG for land, sales-assisted for expand.

## How it works

A simple PLG loop:

```text
Acquire (signup) → Activate (aha moment) → Engage (habit)
        ↑                                     ↓
   Share / invite                      Convert / expand
```

Critical metrics:

- signup → [activation](../06-finance/activation.md) rate
- activation → retention
- free → paid [conversion rate](../06-finance/conversion-rate.md)
- expansion / [net revenue retention](../06-finance/retention.md)
- PQL volume and close rate (if sales-assisted)

### Product-qualified leads (PQLs)

A PQL is an account whose usage suggests buying intent, for example:

- workspace hits 5 active users
- usage crosses a feature gate three times
- team invites admins / billing owner

Sales then helps with security, procurement, and annual contracts — without owning every top-of-funnel click.

## Practical example

A design collaboration tool:

- free individuals create files
- inviting teammates is required to collaborate → acquisition loop
- team workspaces hit seat limits → upgrade
- enterprise needs SSO / SCIM → sales motion

Unit sketch:

- CAC via content + virality: $40
- free-to-paid conversion: 3%
- initial ARPU: $15/user/mo; avg paid workspace = 6 users → $90 [MRR](../06-finance/mrr.md)
- gross margin 85%
- many workspaces expand to 15+ users in year one

Here the product’s collaboration mechanic *is* distribution.

Contrast with a heavy ERP replacement: self-serve PLG may fail; a sales-led motion with implementation partners is more honest.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Freemium | A packaging tactic; PLG is a broader GTM system |
| Product-led sales | Sales triggered by product usage signals |
| Viral growth | One possible PLG mechanism, not required |
| Sales-led growth | Demos and AEs drive most acquisition |
| Growth hacking | Tactics; PLG is a structural motion |

Also: adding a free tier is not PLG if the product cannot deliver value without a salesperson.
