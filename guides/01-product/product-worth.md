# Product Worth

> Product worth is the economic and practical value a product creates for a customer — and therefore what that customer can rationally pay.

## What it means

Product worth answers: “If this product works, how much better off is the customer?”

It is broader than price. Price is what you charge. Worth is the value created through:

- money saved
- money earned
- time saved
- risk reduced
- status / convenience / peace of mind gained

In B2B, worth is often expressible in dollars:

- hours saved × loaded cost per hour
- conversion lift × revenue
- churn prevented × customer value
- error reduction × cost per error

In consumer products, worth may be emotional or habitual, but strong consumer companies still understand substitutes and willingness to pay.

## Why it matters

If you do not understand product worth, you cannot:

- set prices confidently
- write sharp messaging
- prioritize roadmap bets
- explain ROI in sales
- decide how much [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md) you can afford

Many startups undercharge because they price against competitor feature lists instead of against customer outcomes. Others overpromise value they cannot measure.

## When founders should care

- **Before packaging and pricing**
- **When designing a [wedge](wedge.md)** — the wedge should create obvious worth quickly
- **In sales conversations** — especially for ACV above a few thousand dollars
- **When [lead worth](../05-sales/lead-worth.md) and CAC planning begin**
- **At expansion time** — upsells need incremental worth, not just more features

## How it works

### A simple value equation

```text
Product Worth ≈ (Value of outcome) × (Probability product delivers it) − (Cost to adopt)
```

Adoption costs include money, implementation time, training, switching risk, and political risk inside a company.

### B2B value sketch

1. Quantify current pain baseline.
2. Estimate improvement from your product.
3. Convert to annual dollars.
4. Apply a share-of-value price (often customers need to keep most of the value).

Illustrative pricing heuristic (not a rule): if you create $50k/year of measurable value, charging $8k–$15k/year can be easy; charging $45k/year is a harder sell unless value is certain and strategic.

### Time-to-worth

How fast the customer feels value matters as much as total value. A tool that saves $100k/year but takes 9 months to implement can lose to a tool that saves $30k/year in week one — especially for early-stage buyers.

This connects directly to [activation](../06-finance/activation.md).

## Practical example

You sell an AI tool that drafts outbound sales emails for SDR teams.

Customer profile:

- 8 SDRs
- fully loaded cost ≈ $90k/SDR/year
- each SDR spends ~12 hours/week writing personalization

Potential worth:

- time saved: 6 hours/week/SDR × 8 SDRs × 48 weeks = 2,304 hours
- hourly loaded cost ≈ $90,000 / 2,000 hours = $45/hour
- gross time value ≈ 2,304 × $45 ≈ $103,680/year

Then discount for reality:

- only 50% of saved time becomes productive selling time → ~$52k
- adoption friction and editing overhead → maybe $35k–$45k credible ROI story

If annual price is $12k, ROI narrative is strong. If price is $60k, you need stronger proof (pipeline lift, not just time saved).

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Price | What you charge |
| Willingness to pay | What a buyer will actually approve |
| Lead worth | Value of a prospect entering your funnel ([Lead Worth](../05-sales/lead-worth.md)) |
| Features | Inputs; worth is the customer outcome |
| Perceived value | Marketing and proof shape this; still must connect to real outcomes |

Also: founder belief that a product is “worth a lot” is not the same as customer proof.
