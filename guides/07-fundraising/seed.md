# Seed

> Seed is the financing stage focused on getting from early product and initial customers toward repeatable value and a clearer path to product-market fit.

## What it means

Seed capital typically funds:

- product development beyond MVP
- first GTM hires or serious founder-led sales
- infrastructure for early customers
- enough [runway](../06-finance/runway.md) to find a repeatable motion

Instruments: SAFEs/convertibles and priced equity seeds both exist. Ownership outcomes depend on valuation/cap and round size — see [dilution](dilution.md) and [valuation](valuation.md).

Benchmarks for “what Seed companies look like” change with markets. Some Seeds are pre-revenue; others have meaningful ARR. Judge by progress against risk.

## Why it matters

Seed is often the first major external ownership event. It can accelerate learning — or fund denial if spent on scaling before fit.

## When founders should care

- When pre-seed/bootstrapped learning needs a larger surface area
- When you can explain what new evidence the Seed will produce
- When hiring requires a cash buffer beyond personal funds

## How it works

A healthy Seed plan answers:

1. What is the wedge and ICP?
2. What evidence do we have now?
3. What milestones make Series A fundable later?
4. How much runway do we need to reach them with buffer?
5. What will we not spend on yet?

### Milestone examples (illustrative)

- tighten ICP and win 20–40 paying customers
- hit activation/retention thresholds
- prove one acquisition channel
- hire 2–4 critical roles

## Practical example

A team raises a Seed to go from $15k MRR of mixed customers to $60k–$100k MRR in a sharp ICP with improving retention — then run a Series A process. They deliberately avoid hiring a large AE team before conversion playbooks exist.

## Common confusion

Seed is not “Series A but smaller” by default. Series A usually expects a more repeatable growth machine. Also, a large Seed does not eliminate the need for discipline.
