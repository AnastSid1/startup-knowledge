# Valuation

> Valuation is the estimated economic value of a company used to price ownership — what % of the company an investment buys.

## What it means

In venture:

- **Pre-money valuation:** value before new money goes in
- **Post-money valuation:** pre-money + new investment (simplified)

```text
Post-money ≈ Pre-money + New cash invested
Investor ownership ≈ New investment / Post-money
```

Example: $8M pre-money, raise $2M → $10M post-money → investor owns ~20% (before other complexities like option pools).

Early-stage valuations are negotiation + market comps more than DCF precision. Later stages use more metrics (ARR multiples, growth, margins) — still market-dependent.

## Why it matters

Valuation sets dilution and signals. Too high can create pressure and down-round risk. Too low can over-dilute founders/employees. Optimize for a fair outcome that still lets you build — not for vanity headlines.

## When founders should care

- Every priced round
- When setting SAFE caps
- When comparing term sheets (valuation is not the only term)
- When planning employee option pool refreshes

## How it works

### What drives early valuation (practical)

- team
- traction and growth quality
- market size narrative
- competitive heat / capital climate
- scarcity of similar deals
- terms elsewhere in the sheet

### SAFE caps vs priced valuations

A SAFE cap is not identical to a priced round valuation process, but it strongly shapes conversion economics. See [SAFE](safe.md).

## Practical example

Two term sheets:

**A:** $40M pre, heavy preferences, large pool shuffle from founder share  
**B:** $32M pre, cleaner terms, more founder-friendly governance

B can be better. Always model ownership and control, not only headline valuation.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Post-money SAFE ownership | Different mechanics than priced pre/post |
| Dilution | Outcome of valuation + round size + pool |
| Market cap (public) | Different context |
| Revenue × multiple | Heuristic, not a law |
