# Cap Table

> A capitalization table (cap table) is the record of who owns what in a company — equity, options, warrants, and converting instruments.

## What it means

A cap table typically tracks:

- founders’ common shares
- employee option pool (issued and unissued)
- investor preferred shares by round
- SAFEs/notes (converting instruments)
- warrants (if any)
- ownership percentages on fully diluted bases

“Fully diluted” usually assumes options/convertibles are counted in agreed ways — define the basis when you talk percentages.

## Why it matters

The cap table is the ownership map. It affects:

- fundraising negotiations
- hiring offers (option grants)
- governance and voting
- exit proceeds (with preference stacks)

Messy cap tables scare investors and create legal pain.

## When founders should care

- From incorporation / first equity split
- Every financing event
- Every material option grant
- Before diligence

Use proper tools/counsel early; spreadsheets get brittle.

## How it works

### Healthy early practices

1. Clear founder split + vesting (usually 4 years with cliff — norms vary)
2. Reserve an option pool sized to a hiring plan
3. Track every SAFE
4. Keep board approvals organized
5. Model future rounds before signing

### Simple illustration

| Holder | Ownership (fully diluted) |
| --- | --- |
| Founder A | 35% |
| Founder B | 35% |
| Option pool | 10% |
| Seed investors | 20% |

After Series A, these numbers change — model before negotiating.

## Practical example

A startup issued informal advisor grants via email promises without board paperwork. During Series A diligence, cleanup delays the round. Lesson: cap table hygiene is fundraising infrastructure.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Bank account ownership | Irrelevant; equity is legal ownership |
| Dilution | Change in cap table percentages |
| 409A / strike price | Valuation for options; related but distinct |
