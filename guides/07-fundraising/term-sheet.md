# Term Sheet

> A term sheet is a mostly non-binding summary of the key economic and control terms proposed for an investment round.

## What it means

A term sheet outlines the deal before full legal documents are drafted. It usually covers:

**Economics**

- valuation / price
- round size
- option pool size and timing
- liquidation preference
- participation (if any)
- anti-dilution
- dividends (often none practically for early VC)

**Control / governance**

- board seats
- protective provisions / veto rights
- information rights
- founder vesting refresh (sometimes)
- drag/tag along, ROFR (in docs)

Many terms are “standard-ish,” but details matter. Counsel is essential.

> Not legal advice.

## Why it matters

The term sheet is where founders still have negotiating leverage. After signing, the deal often becomes hard to reopen without cost. Headline valuation can hide painful control or preference terms.

## When founders should care

- At every priced round
- When comparing multiple investor offers
- Before celebrating a high valuation alone

## How it works

### Practical review order

1. Ownership math (including pool)
2. Liquidation preference structure
3. Board composition
4. Protective provisions
5. Other constraints on future fundraising/ops

### Liquidation preference (intuition)

1x non-participating preferred is common in many standard early deals: investors get their money back first (or convert to common), not both in a double-dip. Participating preferred can change founder/employee outcomes in moderate exits.

## Practical example

Term sheet A: higher valuation, 2x participating preferred, investor-friendly board control.  
Term sheet B: lower valuation, 1x non-participating, balanced board.

In many moderate exit scenarios, B leaves more for common holders. Model exits at $30M / $100M / $500M before deciding.

## Common confusion

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| “Non-binding means unimportant” | It sets the deal; hard to renegotiate casually |
| “Only valuation matters” | Preferences and control can dominate outcomes |
| “All term sheets are identical” | Not true — read yours |
