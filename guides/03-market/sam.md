# SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)

> SAM is the portion of TAM that your company can actually serve given your product, geography, regulations, and go-to-market constraints today.

## What it means

If [TAM](tam.md) is the whole pie for a category, SAM is the slice you are equipped to sell into now.

Constraints that shrink TAM into SAM:

- language and geography
- integrations you support
- company size your product fits
- regulated vs non-regulated industries
- self-serve vs enterprise sales capability
- platform limitations (e.g., Shopify-only)

SAM should match your real strategy, not your aspiration slide.

## Why it matters

SAM keeps market claims honest. It connects vision to operating reality and helps you allocate product and sales energy.

A startup with a $5B TAM story and a $20M SAM reality should plan like a $20M SAM company first — then show the expansion path.

## When founders should care

- When translating vision into a beachhead plan
- When hiring GTM roles for a specific segment
- When investors ask “who can you sell to now?”
- When deciding which integrations or locales unlock the next SAM expansion

## How it works

```text
SAM ≈ (# of customers you can serve now) × (ARPU you can win now)
```

### Continuing the inventory example

From the [TAM](tam.md) example ($240M):

- You only support Shopify + one 3PL
- You only sell in English-speaking North America
- Product fits brands doing $5M–$80M GMV

Suppose that is 12,000 brands × $6,000 = **$72M SAM**.

That is still meaningful — and much more useful for planning than $240M.

## Practical example

A European payroll startup:

| Layer | Definition | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM | Global SME payroll software spend | Very large |
| SAM | German GmbHs with 5–50 employees needing local compliance | Focused |
| Near-term product | Germany only, DATEV export, German tax rules | Matches SAM |

Expanding to France is not “more marketing.” It is a SAM expansion requiring product and compliance work.

## Common confusion

| Confused with | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| TAM | Broader category potential |
| SOM | Near-term capture target, not capability |
| ICP count | ICP is qualitative/targeting; SAM is market dollars |
