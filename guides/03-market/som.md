# SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)

> SOM is the share of SAM you can realistically capture in a defined near-term period given your team, capital, brand, and distribution.

## What it means

SOM is the practical target, not the dream. It asks: “In the next 3–5 years (or until the next stage), how much revenue can we actually win?”

Good SOM estimates consider:

- sales capacity and conversion rates
- channel access
- competitive intensity
- implementation bandwidth
- capital available for [paid acquisition](../04-growth/paid-acquisition.md)
- time to build trust in the category

SOM should be ambitious enough to matter and grounded enough to operate against.

## Why it matters

SOM turns market sizing into a plan. It informs hiring, fundraising asks, and milestone design.

Example: if your Series A story requires $5M ARR in 24 months, your SOM math should show a believable path from [SAM](sam.md) to that number.

## When founders should care

- Building financial plans and fundraising narratives
- Setting board targets
- Choosing channels that can actually yield the needed volume
- Stress-testing whether a market is too crowded for your resources

## How it works

```text
SOM ≈ SAM × realistic share
```

or bottom-up:

```text
SOM ≈ (capacity of GTM motion) × (win rate) × (ARPU) × (time)
```

### Worked example

SAM = $72M (from [SAM](sam.md) example).

Near-term plan (36 months):

- content + partnerships + 2 AEs
- 40 qualified opps/month by month 24
- 20% close rate
- $7k average ARR
- average customer life considerations aside (this is new ARR booking capacity)

Rough year-3 new bookings capacity:

```text
40 opps/mo × 12 × 20% × $7k ≈ $672k new ARR that year
```

If retention is strong and earlier cohorts accumulate, total ARR could reach ~$1.5M–$2.5M depending on ramp — far below 10% of SAM, which is fine.

Claiming 20% of a $72M SAM in year two with a 6-person team would be a red flag.

| **SOM** | What can we win soon? | Operating + fundraising plan |

## Common confusion

SOM is not a promise. It is a reasoned estimate. Also, SOM is not “1% of TAM” without a capacity model — the lazy 1% heuristic is usually meaningless.
