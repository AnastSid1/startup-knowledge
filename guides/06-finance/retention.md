# Retention

> Retention measures whether users or customers continue to get value and stay over time.

## What it means

Retention can be tracked as:

- **User retention:** % of users still active after N days/weeks
- **Customer retention:** % of customers still paying after N months
- **Revenue retention:** how recurring revenue from a cohort evolves

### Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

```text
NRR ≈ (Starting ARR + Expansion − Contraction − Churn) / Starting ARR
```

for a cohort/base of existing customers over a period (commonly annualized).

NRR > 100% means existing customers grow in value even before new logos.

## Why it matters

Retention is the backbone of [product-market fit](../01-product/product-market-fit.md) evidence and sustainable growth. Acquisition fills the bucket; retention determines whether you have a business.

## When founders should care

- Immediately after first cohorts exist
- Before scaling paid growth
- When prioritizing onboarding and core workflow quality
- In every fundraising narrative after early traction

## How it works

### Cohort retention curves

Plot % retained by weeks since signup. Look for a curve that flattens (habit) rather than decaying to zero.

See [cohorts](cohorts.md).

### Retention vs churn

```text
Retention ≈ 1 − churn   (for the same definition/period)
```

Use consistent definitions (logo vs revenue; monthly vs annual).

## Practical example

A PLG tool’s week-4 retention:

- Broad signups: 12%
- ICP signups who hit activation event: 48%

Product and GTM shift toward the second group. Blended retention rises without a miraculous new feature set — mostly better fit and activation.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Engagement | Depth/frequency; retention is continued presence/payment |
| Churn | Departure rate lens |
| Stickiness (DAU/MAU) | Engagement pattern; not full retention story |
