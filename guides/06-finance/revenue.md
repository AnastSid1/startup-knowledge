# Revenue

> Revenue is the money a company recognizes from customers for goods or services delivered — the top line of the business.

## What it means

In plain terms: customers pay you for value, and revenue is the accounting of that earned amount.

Important distinctions:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | Earned/recognized sales (accounting rules apply) |
| Bookings | Contracted value (may be recognized later) |
| Billings | Invoiced amounts |
| Collections | Cash actually received |
| GMV | Gross marketplace volume (not your take-rate revenue) |

Startups often casually say “revenue” when they mean bookings or ARR. Be precise in board and investor updates.

## Why it matters

Revenue validates willingness to pay. It also feeds [burn](burn-rate.md)/[runway](runway.md) via collections and supports valuation narratives — though revenue quality (retention, margins) matters as much as total amount.

## When founders should care

- From first paid customer
- When designing pricing/packaging
- When reporting growth ([growth rate](growth-rate.md))
- When comparing business models (subscription vs services vs transactions)

## How it works

### SaaS view

Many SaaS startups manage the business with [MRR](mrr.md)/[ARR](arr.md) as operating metrics, while financial statements follow accrual revenue recognition.

### Quality checks

Ask:

- Is revenue recurring or one-off?
- Is it concentrated in a few customers?
- Are discounts masking weak value?
- Does revenue expand or decay in cohorts?

## Practical example

A company signs a $120k annual contract, billed upfront.

- Bookings: $120k
- Cash collection: $120k (if paid)
- Monthly recurring view: $10k MRR
- Recognized revenue: often $10k/month over the year (depending on rules/delivery)

If founders report $120k “revenue” in month one without explanation, they confuse stakeholders.

## Common confusion

Revenue ≠ cash.  
Revenue ≠ fundraising.  
GMV ≠ marketplace revenue.
