# CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)

> CAC is how much it costs, on average, to acquire one new paying customer.

## What it means

```text
CAC = Sales & marketing costs (in a period) / New customers acquired (in that period)
```

Example: spend $50,000 on sales & marketing in a month, win 25 new customers → CAC = $2,000.

Important variants:

- **Blended CAC:** all S&M / all new customers
- **Paid CAC:** paid channel costs / customers from paid
- **CAC by channel/segment:** the useful version for decisions

Include salaries of sales/marketing when you want fully loaded CAC — especially for sales-led motions.

## Why it matters

CAC tells you whether growth is affordable. Paired with [LTV](ltv.md) and [payback](payback-period.md), it drives channel investment and GTM design.

## When founders should care

- Before scaling paid acquisition
- When hiring AEs/SDRs
- In cohort economics reviews
- During fundraising efficiency discussions

Early on, CAC can be noisy. Directional channel comparisons still help.

## How it works

### Fully loaded vs partial

| Partial CAC | Fully loaded CAC |
| --- | --- |
| Ad spend only | Ads + tools + sales/marketing payroll + agencies |
| Useful for creative testing | Necessary for true unit economics |

### Timing mismatch

Sales cycles mean spend in January may create customers in March. Use cohorts or lagged attribution when possible.

## Practical example

Self-serve product:

- ads $20k
- marketing contractor $5k
- new customers 100
- CAC = $250

Sales-led product:

- AE + SDR cost $30k/month
- tools $2k
- ads $8k
- new customers 10
- CAC = $4,000

Both can be fine if LTV and payback differ accordingly.

## Common confusion

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Excluding sales salaries forever | Show fully loaded as you scale |
| Averaging good and bad channels | Segment |
| Using signups as denominator | Use paying customers (or state otherwise) |
