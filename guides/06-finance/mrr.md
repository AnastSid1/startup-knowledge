# MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)

> MRR is the normalized monthly value of recurring subscription revenue.

## What it means

MRR translates subscription contracts into a comparable monthly run-rate.

```text
MRR ≈ sum of normalized monthly subscription amounts
```

If a customer pays $1,200 annually, that typically contributes $100 to MRR.

Common components:

- new MRR
- expansion MRR
- contraction MRR
- churned MRR
- net new MRR

## Why it matters

MRR is the heartbeat metric for subscription startups. It helps you see momentum without waiting for annual accounting cycles and supports forecasting, hiring, and fundraising updates.

## When founders should care

- As soon as you have recurring customers
- In weekly/monthly operating reviews
- When analyzing expansion vs logo growth
- When calculating growth rates and retention

## How it works

### Example bridge

| Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Starting MRR | $80,000 |
| New | +$8,000 |
| Expansion | +$3,000 |
| Contraction | −$1,000 |
| Churn | −$2,500 |
| Ending MRR | $87,500 |
| Net new MRR | $7,500 |

### What to include carefully

- Exclude one-time setup fees from MRR (track separately)
- Be consistent with discounts and free months
- Usage-based companies often track MRR-like “run-rate revenue” with extra care

## Practical example

10 customers × $200/mo = $2,000 MRR.  
Two customers upgrade by $100 each = +$200 expansion.  
One customer cancels ($200) = churn.

Ending MRR = $2,000 + $200 − $200 = $2,000 — flat, even though “activity happened.” The bridge explains why.
