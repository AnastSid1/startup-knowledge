# ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)

> ARR is the annualized value of recurring subscription revenue — often approximated as MRR × 12.

## What it means

```text
ARR ≈ MRR × 12
```

If MRR is $87,500, ARR is about $1.05M.

ARR is an operating metric used heavily in SaaS fundraising and planning. It is not always identical to recognized annual revenue.

Some companies also report **ARR endings** after expansions/churn and distinguish committed ARR vs usage run-rate.

## Why it matters

ARR is a shared language for scale:

- hiring plans
- investor milestones (“path to $1M ARR”)
- valuation comparisons (e.g., multiples of ARR — market-dependent)
- growth rate reporting

Quality of ARR matters: concentrated, heavily discounted, or services-disguised ARR is weaker.

## When founders should care

- From early recurring revenue onward
- Especially around Seed → Series A narratives
- When comparing growth efficiency across periods

## How it works

### ARR building blocks

- beginning ARR
- new ARR
- expansion ARR
- churned/contraction ARR
- ending ARR

### Net revenue retention link

If existing customers expand more than they churn, ARR can grow even without new logos. See [retention](retention.md).

## Practical example

A startup wants Series A credibility.

- 18 months ago: $0
- Today: $1.2M ARR
- Net revenue retention: 115%
- Gross margin: 82%
- Growth: strong MoM earlier, now ~8–10% MoM

The ARR number opens the conversation; retention and margins sustain it.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| [MRR](mrr.md) | Monthly view |
| Run-rate revenue | Sometimes includes non-recurring; define carefully |
| Bookings | Contracted amounts; timing differs |
| GMV | Not ARR |
