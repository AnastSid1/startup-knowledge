# Growth Rate

> Growth rate measures how fast a key metric — often revenue, MRR/ARR, or active users — is increasing over a period.

## What it means

Common forms:

### Month-over-month (MoM)

```text
MoM growth = (This month − Last month) / Last month
```

If MRR goes from $80k to $88k: growth = 10% MoM.

### Year-over-year (YoY)

```text
YoY growth = (This year − Last year) / Last year
```

### Compound intuition

Sustained 15% MoM is extreme and rare at scale. Early startups may see high MoM on small bases; later, growth rates naturally compress as the denominator grows.

Paul Graham’s “Startup = Growth” framing emphasizes growth as a core startup behavior — with judgment about what is being grown (retained paying customers > vanity signups).

## Why it matters

Growth rate affects:

- fundraising outcomes
- hiring urgency
- category momentum
- whether your loops are working

But growth without retention is a leaky bucket. Always pair growth with cohort retention and margins.

## When founders should care

- Weekly/monthly operating cadence
- Board updates
- Channel experiments (did growth quality improve?)
- Stage transitions (Seed vs Series A expectations differ and change with markets)

## How it works

### Choose the right base metric

Prefer:

- net new MRR / ARR
- retained active teams
- successful transactions

Avoid celebrating signup spikes with no activation.

### Growth accounting

Break growth into:

- new
- expansion
- reactivation
- churn/contraction

This shows whether growth is healthy.

## Practical example

Two startups both “grew 8% MoM.”

**A:** +12% new, −4% churn net effect mixed; retention weak.  
**B:** +5% new, +4% expansion, −1% churn — stronger compounding.

Same headline growth; different futures.

## Common confusion

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Growth on tiny base bragging | Contextualize absolute numbers |
| Mixing booked vs recognized | Define metric |
| Ignoring seasonality | Use trailing averages / YoY |
| Growth at all costs | Watch CAC and retention |
