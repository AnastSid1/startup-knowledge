# Burn Rate

> Burn rate is how quickly a company is spending its cash — usually measured as net cash consumed per month.

## What it means

There are two common versions:

| Type | Meaning | Formula sketch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross burn | Total monthly cash operating spend | Cash outflows / month |
| Net burn | Monthly cash loss after incoming cash | Cash out − cash in (approx.) |

Example: spend $200k/month, collect $50k/month → net burn ≈ $150k/month.

Investors usually care most about **net burn** and the resulting [runway](runway.md).

## Why it matters

Burn determines survival. It also signals operating discipline and how aggressively you are investing in growth.

High burn is not automatically bad (if growth and learning justify it). High burn with weak retention usually is.

## When founders should care

- Always while unprofitable
- Before hiring waves
- When planning a fundraise (burn drives how much to raise)
- When revenue scales but spend scales faster

## How it works

Track burn from cash, not only accrual accounting. Include:

- payroll and contractors
- infrastructure
- ads
- tools
- taxes/payments timing if material

### Practical controls

1. Separate growth experiments from fixed burn
2. Hire in cohorts tied to milestones
3. Review burn monthly against plan
4. Know default-alive vs default-dead (PG framing)

## Practical example

Cash in bank: $1.8M  
Monthly gross spend: $220k  
Monthly cash receipts: $70k  
Net burn: $150k

If receipts rise to $120k and spend stays flat, net burn falls to $100k — runway extends without raising.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Expenses on P&L | May include non-cash items; burn is cash |
| Gross vs net burn | Don’t mix them in board updates |
| Runway | Time left; burn is the speed of spend |
