# Business Model

> A business model explains how a company creates value for customers and captures enough of that value to build a sustainable business.

## What it means

Your business model is the system connecting:

- who pays
- what they pay for
- how value is delivered
- what it costs to deliver
- how you acquire customers
- how money flows over time

Common startup models:

| Model | Customer pays for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription SaaS | Access over time | Predictable [ARR](../06-finance/arr.md) |
| Usage-based | Consumption | Aligns with value; revenue more variable |
| Transaction / take-rate | Each transaction | Marketplaces, payments |
| One-time + services | License/setup + services | Can hurt scalability if services dominate |
| Advertising | Attention (advertisers pay) | Needs scale; user vs advertiser tension |
| Freemium hybrid | Upgrades / teams | Common in [PLG](../01-product/product-led-growth.md) |

A pitch deck “model” slide is not enough. The model must survive contact with [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md), [gross margin](../06-finance/gross-margin.md), and retention reality.

## Why it matters

Great products still fail with broken models: acquisition too expensive, margins too thin, or value capture mismatched to value creation ([product worth](../01-product/product-worth.md)).

Business model choices also constrain GTM. A $29/mo tool rarely supports a heavy field-sales motion. A $80k ACV enterprise product rarely thrives on pure self-serve alone.

## When founders should care

- Choosing packaging and pricing early
- Deciding sales motion ([sales motion](../05-sales/sales-motion.md))
- Planning fundraising (capital needs differ by model)
- Revisiting after PMF when expansion revenue becomes available

## How it works

Map the loop:

```text
Value created → packaging → price → acquisition cost → delivery cost → retention/expansion → profit
```

Stress-test with questions:

1. Who is the economic buyer?
2. Is purchase frequent or one-time?
3. Does revenue expand naturally with usage or seats?
4. Are gross margins high enough for software-like scaling?
5. How long until payback on CAC? (see [payback period](../06-finance/payback-period.md))

## Practical example

A workflow product can pick different models:

- **Seat subscription:** $40/user/mo — good if collaboration expands seats
- **Usage-based:** $0.03 per automated run — good if value tracks volume
- **Platform fee + usage:** base $500/mo + usage — stabilizes revenue while aligning to value

If typical customers start with 3 users and grow to 20, seat-based expansion may outperform a flat monthly fee.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue model | How you charge; business model includes costs and acquisition system |
| Pitch narrative | Story; model must be operationally true |
| Growth strategy | How you get customers; model includes how you make money from them |
