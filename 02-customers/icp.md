# ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

> An ICP describes the type of customer who gets exceptional value from your product, is reachable, and can buy in a way that supports a healthy business.

## What it means

Your Ideal Customer Profile is a practical definition of “best-fit customer,” usually at the account or segment level (especially in B2B), complemented by buyer and user personas.

A useful ICP includes:

- firmographics / demographics (industry, size, geo, stage)
- situation triggers (what changed that creates urgency)
- jobs to be done
- current alternatives and budget owner
- ability to succeed with your product
- economics that work for both sides

Example ICP one-liner:

> “B2B SaaS companies with 20–100 employees, $1M–$10M ARR, using HubSpot, where a Head of CS owns onboarding completion and currently stitches together spreadsheets + Notion.”

That is actionable. “Any company that wants better customer success” is not.

## Why it matters

ICP clarity improves almost every function:

- product prioritization
- messaging and [positioning](../08-strategy/positioning.md)
- outbound and content targeting
- sales qualification
- lower [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md)
- higher retention and expansion

Without an ICP, teams optimize for anyone who will take a meeting. That creates noisy feedback and weak [product-market fit](../01-product/product-market-fit.md) signals.

## When founders should care

- **Before building much:** who is this for?
- **When early customers are too mixed:** tighten before scaling spend.
- **When hiring sales:** AEs need a repeatable target.
- **When expanding markets:** define a new ICP explicitly instead of blurring the old one.

Your first ICP can be wrong. Update it from win/loss and retention data.

## How it works

### Build an ICP from evidence

1. List your best customers (or best design partners).
2. Find shared attributes.
3. Note who churned or stalled — anti-ICP traits.
4. Write inclusion and exclusion criteria.
5. Test acquisition channels against that definition.
6. Revise quarterly in early stages.

### Jobs to be done (brief)

Ask what progress the customer is trying to make:

- functional job (“close the books faster”)
- emotional job (“not dread month-end”)
- social job (“look competent to the board”)

Your product should map to the job, not only to a feature checklist.

### ICP vs persona vs segment

| Term | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| ICP | Best-fit account/customer type for the business |
| Persona | Human roles: user, champion, economic buyer |
| Segment | Broader market slice; ICP is the sharp target within/among segments |

## Practical example

A startup sells inventory forecasting.

**Broad (weak):** retailers who want better inventory.

**Sharp ICP:**

- DTC brands on Shopify
- $8M–$40M GMV
- 200–2,000 SKUs
- currently overselling/stockouts monthly
- Head of Ops or Head of Supply Chain owns tools budget
- can integrate Shopify + one 3PL

**Anti-ICP:**

- single-SKU stores
- marketplaces with no inventory ownership
- enterprises requiring 9-month RFPs in year one

Early GTM:

- content and outbound only to that ICP
- case studies from that ICP
- roadmap ignores enterprise SSO until ICP retention is strong

Result: fewer leads, higher close rate, clearer product learning.

## Common confusion

| Confused with | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Total market | [TAM](../03-market/tam.md) is potential size; ICP is who you serve now |
| Anyone who will pay | Early revenue from bad-fit customers can hurt |
| Persona alone | Knowing “Sarah the manager” without firm constraints is incomplete |
| Beachhead | Beachhead is the initial market focus; ICP defines the best customer inside it |
