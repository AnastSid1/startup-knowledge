# Wedge

> A wedge is the narrow product, customer, or use case you start with so you can win a small beachhead before expanding.

## What it means

Most startups cannot attack an entire market at once. A wedge is your sharp entry point: a specific problem for a specific customer that you can dominate quickly.

A wedge can be:

- a **feature wedge** — one workflow done better than incumbents (Dropbox’s simple file sync early on)
- a **customer wedge** — one segment ignored by bigger players (serving freelancers before enterprises)
- a **geo / channel wedge** — one city, platform, or community first
- a **price / packaging wedge** — a simpler, cheaper, or self-serve version of a complex category

The wedge is not your final vision. It is the first territory you can realistically own.

## Why it matters

Broad products sound impressive in pitch decks and fail in sales calls.

A strong wedge helps you:

- learn faster with a clear [ICP](../02-customers/icp.md)
- build a sharper [MVP](mvp.md)
- create word-of-mouth inside a dense community
- win deals against larger, more general products
- create a path to expand into adjacent use cases later

Investors and operators often ask: “Where do you win first?” The wedge is the answer.

## When founders should care

- **Idea stage:** before building a platform for everyone.
- **Early GTM:** when messaging feels vague (“we help companies be more efficient”).
- **Fundraising:** when explaining how a tiny team beats incumbents in one place.
- **Expansion stage:** when deciding the next wedge after the first one works.

If you cannot name the wedge in one sentence, your product and GTM are probably too diffuse.

## How it works

A useful wedge test:

1. **Is the pain acute?** Users need a fix now, not “someday.”
2. **Is the buyer reachable?** Can you find and talk to them this month?
3. **Can you be 10x better on one job?** Not slightly better on everything.
4. **Is there a density effect?** Will users talk to each other?
5. **Is there a natural expansion path?** After winning this job, what is next?

### Example expansion path

```text
Wedge: AI replies for ecommerce support tickets
  → adjacent: macros + help center generation
    → adjacent: full support workspace for mid-market ecommerce
      → later: multi-channel CX platform
```

The vision can be large. The entry cannot.

## Practical example

**Vision:** “AI operating system for mid-market operations teams.”

That is not a wedge.

**Wedge options:**

| Wedge | Why it can work | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Returns triage for Shopify brands doing $5M–$50M GMV | Clear pain, similar stack, reachable buyers | May be niche if expansion is weak |
| Invoice chasing for EU agencies | High urgency, measurable ROI | Smaller ACV |
| Onboarding checklist tool for Series A SaaS CS teams | Founder knows buyers; dense network | Market may be crowded |

Suppose you pick Shopify returns triage:

- ICP: Head of Ops / Support at Shopify Plus brands
- Promise: cut returns handling time by 40%
- MVP: intake → classify → route → draft customer update
- Distribution: Shopify app store, Twitter/X Shopify operators, partner agencies

Once 50 customers retain and expand, you can move into shipping exceptions, then broader ops automation.

## Common confusion

| Concept | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| MVP | How small/simple the product is; wedge is *where* you enter |
| Niche | Can be permanent; a wedge is often temporary on purpose |
| Beachhead market | Closely related; beachhead is usually the market segment, wedge includes product angle too |
| Positioning | How you are understood relative to alternatives; wedge is the strategic entry choice |
| Roadmap | Future features; wedge is the first winning battle |

A niche with no expansion path can be a lifestyle business. A wedge with a clear adjacency map can become a large company.
