# Distribution

> Distribution is how your product reliably reaches customers — the channels and mechanisms that create ongoing access to demand.

## What it means

A product without distribution is a secret. Distribution includes:

- direct sales
- self-serve acquisition
- marketplaces and app stores
- partnerships and integrations
- content and community
- paid channels
- viral / embedded product sharing

Peter Thiel and others have argued that distribution is often harder than building the product. Many startups overbuild and under-distribute.

## Why it matters

Better technology does not automatically win. The company that owns attention, partnerships, or default placement can beat a superior product that nobody sees.

Distribution strategy should match [ICP](../02-customers/icp.md), ACV, and [business model](../08-strategy/business-model.md).

## When founders should care

- From the first [MVP](../01-product/mvp.md): how will 20 users find this?
- When planning GTM hiring
- When choosing platforms to build on (Shopify, Slack, AWS Marketplace, etc.)
- When evaluating defensibility ([brand](../03-market/brand-moat.md), networks, partnerships)

## How it works

Map distribution as a system:

1. **Where attention already is** for your ICP
2. **What trust transfer exists** (partners, communities, marketplaces)
3. **What your product can embed into** (integrations, sharing)
4. **What you can afford** given [CAC](../06-finance/cac.md) and payback

### Channel examples by motion

| Motion | Distribution examples |
| --- | --- |
| PLG | App stores, templates, viral invites, SEO |
| Sales-led | Outbound, partners, events, founder-led sales |
| Hybrid | Inbound PQLs + AE assist |

## Practical example

A Slack bot for incident management:

- distribution via Slack Marketplace listing
- content for SRE communities
- open-source utility that leads to cloud upgrade
- later, partnerships with observability vendors

The “product” includes marketplace presence and integration depth — not only the bot logic.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing | Activities; distribution is the reliable path to customers |
| Growth hacking | Tactics that may not compound |
| Virality | One distribution mechanism |
| Sales | One distribution motion |
