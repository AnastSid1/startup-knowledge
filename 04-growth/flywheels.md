# Flywheels

> A flywheel is a self-reinforcing strategic loop where each success makes the next success easier, building momentum over time.

## What it means

Jim Collins popularized the flywheel metaphor in company building: you push a heavy wheel until momentum takes over. In startups, flywheels describe compounding systems across product, distribution, and data.

Example Amazon-style narrative (simplified):

```text
lower prices → more customers → more sellers → better selection → more customers → greater scale → lower costs → lower prices
```

Startup flywheels are often less grand at first, but the idea is the same: design the business so outputs become inputs.

## Why it matters

Flywheels help teams prioritize investments that compound rather than one-off spikes. They also communicate strategy clearly: “If we improve X, Y gets easier, which improves Z.”

## When founders should care

- After identifying a real [growth loop](growth-loops.md)
- When writing strategy docs or Series A narratives
- When choosing between short-term hacks and compounding assets
- When connecting product, GTM, and [moat](../03-market/moat.md) building

## How it works

A useful flywheel has:

1. 3–6 concrete steps
2. each step causally helping the next
3. at least one step that strengthens defensibility
4. metrics for friction points

### Flywheel vs growth loop

| | Flywheel | Growth loop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Altitude | Strategy narrative | Operational mechanism |
| Scope | Often company-wide | Often one acquisition/retention engine |
| Use | Alignment and prioritization | Experimentation and optimization |

You can have multiple loops powering one flywheel.

## Practical example

Vertical SaaS flywheel:

```text
Win niche customers → create category templates → faster onboarding for next customers
→ better benchmarks/data → stronger product → more references → easier sales → more customers
```

Investment choices:

- templates team (speeds onboarding)
- benchmark reports (data advantage)
- customer advisory group (references)

Not every feature request belongs; only pushes that accelerate the wheel.

## Common confusion

Flywheels are not magic posters. If causal links are fake, you have a slogan. Also, flywheels still need an initial push — usually founder-led sales, capital, or an acute wedge.
