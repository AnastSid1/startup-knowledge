# Viral Loops

> A viral loop is a growth loop where users expose or invite others as a natural part of getting value from the product, creating new users who repeat the cycle.

## What it means

Viral loops are a specific type of [growth loop](growth-loops.md) powered by [virality](virality.md).

Typical structure:

```text
User joins → reaches value → shares/invites as part of workflow → new user joins → ...
```

Examples:

- sending a payment link that exposes the product
- inviting teammates to a shared workspace
- publishing a public profile/page
- multiplayer gameplay invites

## Why it matters

Viral loops can create efficient acquisition because distribution is embedded in product usage. They also create clear product requirements: the share moment must be natural and valuable for both sides.

## When founders should care

- Collaboration, communication, marketplace, and creator tools
- [PLG](../01-product/product-led-growth.md) onboarding design
- When invite mechanics already appear in user behavior qualitatively

## How it works

Instrument the loop stages and optimize the bottleneck.

| Stage | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Time-to-share | How fast do users hit a share-worthy moment? |
| Share rate | What % share/invite? |
| Recipient conversion | Do recipients understand value quickly? |
| Recipient activation | Do they become real users? |
| Repeat | Do they create the next share? |

Compare to [referral loops](referral-loops.md): viral loops are usually intrinsic to product value; referral loops often add explicit incentives.

## Practical example

A contract tool’s viral loop:

1. User creates a contract
2. Sends it to a counterparty to sign
3. Counterparty experiences the product
4. Counterparty later creates their own contracts

Product investments: beautiful recipient experience, frictionless signup at the moment of value, templates for the counterparty’s industry.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Referral loop | Incentive-led invites |
| Network effects | Value increases with users; may or may not drive viral acquisition |
| Growth loop | Parent category |
| Flywheel | Higher-level strategy narrative |
