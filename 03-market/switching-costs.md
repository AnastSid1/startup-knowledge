# Switching Costs

> Switching costs are the time, money, risk, and friction a customer faces when moving from your product to an alternative.

## What it means

If leaving is expensive — financially, operationally, or emotionally — customers stay longer even when competitors look attractive.

Sources of switching costs:

- data migration and historical records
- workflow redesign and retraining
- integrations and custom configuration
- contractual commitments
- political risk (“if the new tool fails, I get blamed”)
- community / identity / content locked into a platform
- re-implementing compliance and security reviews

High switching costs can support retention and a [moat](moat.md), but only if customers were happy enough to get embedded in the first place. Trapping unhappy users creates churn debt and reputation damage.

## Why it matters

Switching costs influence:

- [churn](../06-finance/churn.md) and [LTV](../06-finance/ltv.md)
- sales cycles for replacements (you must overcome incumbent friction)
- pricing power
- competitive defense

For startups attacking incumbents, understanding *their* switching costs is as important as building your own.

## When founders should care

- Designing onboarding and system-of-record features
- Pricing annual contracts vs monthly
- Building integrations and migration tools (to reduce switching costs *into* you)
- Evaluating defensibility after PMF

## How it works

Map the customer’s leave process:

1. Export data
2. Rebuild automations
3. Retrain staff
4. Reconnect integrations
5. Re-earn internal trust
6. Run old/new systems in parallel

Each step is a cost. Your retention strategy can deepen valuable embed points — while your acquisition strategy should minimize those costs for prospects leaving someone else.

### Ethical framing

Good switching costs come from genuine accumulated value (history, workflows, networks). Bad ones come from dark patterns and hostage data. The first builds trust; the second invites regulation and backlash.

## Practical example

A HRIS embeds:

- employee records
- time-off policies
- payroll mappings
- approval chains
- audit history

Even if a competitor is 20% nicer UI, switching may cost six months and significant error risk. That supports retention.

A simple single-player Chrome extension with no data history has low switching costs — competitors can win with a slightly better feature and a coupon.

## Common confusion

| Term | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| Lock-in (pejorative) | Often implies unfair friction |
| Retention | Outcome; switching costs are one cause |
| Network effects | Value rises with users; switching costs raise leave friction |
| Contracts alone | Legal lock-in without value is fragile |
