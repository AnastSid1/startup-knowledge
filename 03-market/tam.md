# TAM (Total Addressable Market)

> TAM is the total annual revenue opportunity if your product captured 100% of a defined market for a specific offer.

## What it means

TAM answers: “If every possible customer bought our type of solution, how large is that market in dollars per year?”

It is a definition exercise as much as a math exercise. A vague market (“AI for business”) produces a fantasy TAM. A precise offer (“scheduling software for multi-location dental clinics in the US”) produces a useful one.

Two common methods:

1. **Top-down:** start from industry research, then narrow.
2. **Bottom-up:** count target customers × annual spend per customer.

Bottom-up is usually more credible for early startups.

## Why it matters

TAM helps founders and investors understand whether a business can become large if things go well. It also forces clarity about what category you are really in.

TAM alone does not justify a startup. A huge TAM with no [wedge](../01-product/wedge.md) still fails. A smaller TAM with expansion adjacencies can still build a big company.

## When founders should care

- Pitching and strategy documents
- Choosing among startup ideas
- Deciding whether a niche is a dead end or a beachhead
- Sanity-checking pricing × customer count math

Do not spend weeks polishing TAM slides before talking to customers.

## How it works

### Bottom-up formula

```text
TAM ≈ (# of relevant customers) × (annual revenue per customer if they buy your category)
```

Be explicit about:

- geography
- customer type
- product category boundaries
- whether figures are today’s spend or future spend

### Worked example

Offer: B2B SaaS for inventory forecasting for US DTC brands.

Assumptions:

- 40,000 US DTC brands in a relevant GMV band
- category spend if they buy a forecasting tool: $6,000 ARR average

```text
TAM = 40,000 × $6,000 = $240M ARR
```

If you later expand into wholesale brands and retailers, your long-term category TAM may be larger — but label that as expansion TAM, not the current one.

## Common confusion

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| [SAM](sam.md) | The part of TAM you can serve with your product/GTM constraints |
| [SOM](som.md) | The share you can realistically capture near-term |
| “Market size” slides with no method | Often theater |

Also avoid counting every company worldwide at enterprise ACV when you sell a $49/mo tool.
